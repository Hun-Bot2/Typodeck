Northwind Roasters is a twelve-store specialty coffee chain in the Pacific Northwest. After eight years of wholesale-led growth, the founders want to open a flagship roastery and tasting room in Seattle. The flagship costs 480,000 dollars to build, seats 90 guests, and roasts 40,000 pounds of green coffee per year on a refurbished 25-kilo Probat drum roaster installed in 2019.

The business case rests on three numbers. First, existing stores average 1.9 million dollars in annual revenue at an 18 percent contribution margin. Second, the flagship is projected to add 2.4 million dollars in year-one revenue, with 35 percent from on-site retail and brewing classes. Third, the company already has 6,200 loyalty members within five miles of the proposed site, and those members visit 3.1 times per month on average.

The team has signed a ten-year lease with two five-year options, secured a 5,000-square-foot corner unit on Pike Street, and hired a head roaster who placed third in the 2024 United States Roasting Championship. Construction takes seven months. The ask is 480,000 dollars in growth capital, repaid from flagship cash flow over five years, with investor updates every quarter and an open-cupping invitation every harvest season.
