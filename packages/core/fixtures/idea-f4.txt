Pantrylog is a pantry inventory app for busy households that keep losing track of what they already own. The average test household wastes about 40 percent of the fresh food it buys, roughly 80 dollars a month, mostly because items expire silently at the back of a shelf. Pantrylog fixes this with a 90-second weekly ritual: scan or photograph groceries as they come home, and the app tracks all 12 storage zones across the fridge, freezer, and pantry cabinets.

The product is designed for households of 3 to 5 people who cook at least 4 dinners a week. Onboarding takes under 5 minutes: the household creates a shared pantry, adds its usual 60 staple products from a catalog of more than 500 common groceries, and sets a weekly cooking goal. Each item carries a predicted shelf life, for example 7 days for milk and 21 days for carrots, and the app sends a nudge 2 days before the predicted expiry date.

The core loop has three steps. First, capture: the phone camera or a 15-dollar Bluetooth scanner logs items in about 3 seconds each. Second, plan: every Sunday morning the app proposes 5 dinner ideas built from what is already in stock, prioritizing the 8 items closest to expiry. Third, shop: the generated list covers only the missing ingredients, typically 6 to 9 items, and syncs to any of the 4 supported grocery delivery services.

Business model is a flat subscription of 6 dollars per month per household, with a 30-day free trial. Pilot data from 25 households over 8 weeks shows a 32 percent drop in expired items and 45 dollars in average monthly savings, which pays for the subscription more than 7 times over. Support load is light: only 11 tickets in the whole pilot, and 9 of them were feature requests.

The roadmap has 3 phases. Phase one adds barcode coverage from 500 to 2000 products and a Spanish-language interface for the 18 percent of pilot users who asked for it. Phase two introduces a 20-dollar smart shelf sensor that weighs staple jars and reorders automatically at 25 percent remaining. Phase three explores partnerships with 10 regional grocery chains for receipt-level import. The team of 4 founders brings 30 combined years across food retail, mobile apps, and logistics, and the plan is to reach 10000 paying households within 18 months of launch.

Competition falls into 3 buckets: generic list apps with over 50 million downloads but no expiry tracking, smart-fridge vendors whose 3000-dollar hardware reaches fewer than 2 percent of kitchens, and recipe sites that ignore inventory entirely. The main risks are 2-fold: capture fatigue if logging takes longer than 90 seconds a week, and catalog gaps in the 14 ethnic grocery categories the pilot flagged. Both are addressed by the scanner accessory and a community catalog program targeting 5000 products by month 12.
