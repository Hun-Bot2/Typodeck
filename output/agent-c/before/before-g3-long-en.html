POLICY BRIEF: HARBOR LEVY
P7.3
WHY THE LEVY FAILED
The harbor levy failed twice: once at the ballot box and once more in committee, and the two failures have different causes that are usually conflated in press coverage. Understanding each separately is the only way to draft a third attempt that survives contact with the harbor board, the fishing cooperatives, and the state auditors.

A coalition that never met
The levy needed dockworkers and merchants to campaign together. They never shared a room: the merchants met downtown on Tuesdays while the dockworkers met at the hall on Thursdays, and neither group trusted the consultants who carried messages between them. Turnout collapsed in exactly the precincts the levy needed most.
★
Numbers that moved
Revenue projections changed three times during the campaign as fuel prices swung. Each revision gave opponents a fresh headline, and the final estimate landed the week before the vote, too late to explain. Voters remembered the revisions, not the reasons.
★
The audit nobody read
A favorable state audit arrived in August and sat unread while the committee debated procedure. By the time anyone quoted it, the narrative had hardened. Timing beat substance.
